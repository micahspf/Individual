# Custom manufacturing catalog

What Individual can actually make, organized by **process and material** rather than by
finished product — because that is how a custom-work buyer describes what they need
("can you engrave stainless?"), not by browsing a gift catalog.

Doubles as an intake checklist: each entry lists what to ask for before quoting.
Structured so it can seed a real app catalog when the shop returns.

> **First piece may be free.** On custom projects and prototypes the first sample is on
> me — ask when you send the details. Marked ⚡ below where it applies.

---

## 1. Laser engraving

Permanent marking on hard surfaces. Highest-volume, fastest-turnaround work.

| Material | Typical pieces | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Stainless steel | Tumblers, bottles, can coolers, flasks | Marks light-on-dark; powder-coated blanks engrave to the metal underneath |
| Wood (basswood, birch, maple) | Signs, cutting boards, coasters, plaques | Grain affects contrast — hardwood burns crisper than soft |
| Slate | Coasters, memorial markers, house numbers | Outdoor-safe, high contrast, brittle at thin sections |
| Acrylic | Name plates, awards, signage, light panels | Cast engraves frosted-white; extruded goes clear-glossy |
| Leather | Tags, patches, straps, wallets | Veg-tan takes the darkest mark; test any dyed stock first |
| Anodized aluminum | Tags, plates, panels, pet ID | Marking strips the anodizing to bright silver |

**Ask before quoting:** exact wording (spelling matters — engraved is permanent) ·
size in inches · one-off or quantity · material supplied or sourced · font/style
preference · deadline.

---

## 2. 3D printing ⚡

Functional plastic parts to your measurements. This is where the free-first-sample offer
earns its keep — nobody commits to a run before holding a fit check.

| Category | Typical work | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Replacement parts | Discontinued knobs, clips, brackets, housings, handles | Strongest pitch: the part nobody sells anymore |
| Prototypes ⚡ | Proof-of-fit models, form studies, design iterations | First sample free — that is how the relationship starts |
| Jigs and fixtures ⚡ | Drill guides, alignment tools, assembly aids, templates | Sells to shops and manufacturers, not consumers |
| Flexible pieces | Gaskets, grips, bumpers, articulated toys | TPU — softer, needs slower print, price accordingly |
| Multi-part assemblies | Enclosures, hinged boxes, print-in-place mechanisms | Quote per part, assemble before shipping |
| Small-batch production | 10–200 identical parts | Cheaper per unit than one-off; ask about volume up front |

**Materials:** PLA (rigid, indoor, cheapest) · PETG (tougher, some heat and UV
tolerance) · TPU (flexible) · ABS/ASA (outdoor and heat, on request).

**Ask before quoting:** dimensions, or the broken original to measure · indoor or
outdoor · does it bear load or heat · quantity · color · deadline. **A photo with a
tape measure next to the part answers most of this.**

---

## 3. One-off commissions

The catch-all. Usually starts as "can you even do this?" — the answer is a quote, not a
yes or no.

- Memorials and keepsakes — garden markers, plaques, urn plates
- Wedding and event runs — favors, signage, table numbers, gift sets
- Corporate — branded drinkware, awards, desk pieces, onboarding gifts
- Lake and property signs — dock names, house numbers, coordinates
- Repairs and adaptations — bring the broken thing
- Combination pieces — engraved wood on a printed base, and similar

**Ask before quoting:** what it is for · a photo or sketch · rough size · quantity ·
hard deadline (event dates are real deadlines) · budget range, if they will say.

## What is not offered

Say this plainly rather than quoting badly and regretting it:

- ❌ Injection molding or any real mass production
- ❌ Metal fabrication — welding, machining, casting
- ❌ Electronics assembly or PCB work
- ❌ Textiles, embroidery, screen printing
- ❌ Food-contact items requiring certification
- ❌ Anything needing a licensed trade

---

## Pricing posture

No fixed price list — every job is quoted. The written rule:

1. Quote before anything is produced. Always.
2. First sample free on prototypes and custom parts (⚡ above), not on gift items.
3. Small batches priced per unit, one-offs priced per job.
4. Rush work is a real surcharge, not a favor — say so at quote time.
5. Standard turnaround is 7–10 days; commit to a date only after seeing the design.
