# Testimonial + case study request

**Ask between day 21 and day 30.** Not later. At day 21 the work is fresh and the
relationship is in its best shape. At day 60 you're background noise.

Ask only when at least one of these is true:
- Cost per booked call is at or under the target in Section 8 of the agreement
- The client has told you they booked jobs from the ads
- Something concrete improved — wasted spend cut, calls up, cost per call down

**If none of those are true, don't ask.** Fix the account first. A lukewarm testimonial
is worse than none, and asking for one during a bad month damages the renewal.

---

## The in-call ask (preferred)

Do this at the end of the day-30 review, right after you've shown a good number.

> "One thing before we wrap. When we started, the deal was a lower rate in exchange for
> using the results as a case study. Would you be up for a couple sentences I can use?
> I'll draft it from what you've already told me and send it over — you change anything
> you want or scrap it entirely."

**Drafting it for them is the whole trick.** "Can you write me a testimonial" becomes a
homework assignment and dies. "I'll write it, you approve it" gets a yes almost every
time.

## The email ask

**Subject:** `quick favor — 2 minutes`

```
[First],

Month one's in the books — 34 calls at $41 each, and you said 9 of them
booked. That's the number we set out to hit.

When we started I gave you the lower rate in exchange for using the results
as a case study. Mind if I do that now?

Two things, both easy:

1. Can I use [Company]'s name and these numbers?
2. Three questions below — one line each is plenty. I'll turn them into
   something you approve before it goes anywhere.

- What was going on with your marketing before we started?
- What's different now?
- Would you tell another HVAC owner to do this? Why?

Thanks — [Your name]
```

### Why those three questions

They produce a **before → after → recommendation** arc, which is the only structure that
reads as credible. Asking "how was working with me?" gets you "great, very responsive" —
which proves nothing and persuades nobody.

## Turning answers into a usable quote

Their raw answers will be blunt and unpolished. Keep it that way — tighten the grammar,
cut the filler, don't add adjectives.

**Weak (what you get if you ask badly):**
> "Micah was great to work with and very responsive."

**Strong (what the three questions produce):**
> "We were spending about $900 a month on Angi leads and half of them were tire
> kickers. First month on Google Ads we booked nine jobs, two of them full system
> replacements. Cost per call went from wherever it was to $41 and I can actually see
> where the money went."
> — *Owner, HVAC company, Madison AL*

Anonymized is fine if they're skittish about competitors. "Owner, 6-truck HVAC company,
Huntsville metro" still works — the numbers carry it.

## The case study — one page

```
[Company] — Huntsville, AL

The situation
[2 sentences. What was happening before.]

What we did
[3 bullets. Specific and boring. "Split repair and replacement into separate
campaigns so replacement budget stopped getting eaten by cheaper repair clicks."]

The first 30 days
Ad spend: $X
Calls: X
Cost per call: $X
Booked jobs: X
Revenue from booked jobs: $X

In their words
"[quote]"
```

**Rules:**
- Real numbers or none. Never round up, never estimate.
- Show the ad spend. Hiding it makes the result look fabricated.
- One page. Screenshot of the reporting sheet at the bottom.
- Client sees it before anyone else does. Always.

## Also ask for the referral — same conversation

> "Last thing — do you know another HVAC owner outside your service area who'd want
> this? I only work with one company per market, so I'd rather it come from you than
> cold."

"Outside your service area" is what makes this askable. It removes the competitive
objection entirely, and HVAC owners know each other across metros through supply houses
and distributor events.

---

## Done when

- [ ] Written permission to use their name and numbers
- [ ] A quote with a real before/after in it
- [ ] One-page case study drafted and approved by the client
- [ ] Case study on the one-pager
- [ ] Referral asked for

**When this checklist is complete, the first sale is officially successful:** money
collected, work delivered, value acknowledged in writing.
