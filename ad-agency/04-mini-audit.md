# Mini-audit template — your Phase 2 proof

**30 minutes each. $0. No client and no ad budget required.**

## Why audits instead of a self-funded campaign

You have no ad budget and no prior results. The other proof route — running your own
HVAC campaign — means paying real money per click in a metro where clicks aren't cheap,
for leads you can't service and a result no client can verify. Audits cost nothing,
take half an hour, and double as your outreach opener.

**Do 3 before you send outreach. Then do one for every prospect who replies.**

---

## What to capture — 6 screenshots, in this order

Do all of it **on your phone.** HVAC search is overwhelmingly mobile and emergency, and
a desktop audit will miss the actual problems.

| # | Screenshot | What you're looking for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Google results for `emergency ac repair [their city]` | Are they there? Paid, organic, map pack, or nowhere? Who's above them? |
| 2 | Google results for `ac replacement [their city]` | Replacement is the $10k ticket. Most competitors ignore it. |
| 3 | Their ad, zoomed in | Call extension? Sitelinks? Location? Or a bare headline? |
| 4 | The page the ad lands on, above the fold | Phone number visible without scrolling? Or a hero image and a menu? |
| 5 | Mobile tap-to-call test | Tap the number. Does it dial? Screenshot the dialer — or the failure. |
| 6 | PageSpeed Insights, mobile score | `pagespeed.web.dev` — mobile score and Largest Contentful Paint |

**Also do, and don't screenshot:** call the business during working hours. Note who
answered, how fast, and whether it was a human. This is the single most important
finding in most HVAC audits and it never shows up in a tool.

---

## The 5 fix prompts

Write one short paragraph on each. Plain language — the reader runs an HVAC company,
not a marketing department.

**1. Where the money is going that shouldn't be.**
Broad match with no negatives, ads running when nobody answers, spend on "hvac jobs" or
"hvac school" or DIY terms. Name the specific waste.

**2. Where the click lands.**
Ad → homepage is the most common HVAC mistake there is. Repair ads should hit a repair
page with the phone number in the top inch.

**3. Whether the phone actually gets answered.**
What happened when you called. If it rang out, this is finding #1 and everything else
is secondary — say so plainly.

**4. What competitors say that they don't.**
Financing, same-day, 24/7, licensed, warranty length, free estimate. Pull the exact
phrases from two competitors' live ads.

**5. The one thing to do this week.**
One action. Not a list. Specific enough to do without you.

---

## Format

Two pages, PDF, no logo required. Header:

```
[Company Name] — Google Ads snapshot
Prepared [date] · No charge · Not a proposal
```

Then: **What I looked at** (one line) → **What I found** (5 items, ranked by money) →
**The one thing I'd do this week** → **What I do** (three lines, your offer sentence).

Screenshots inline next to each finding. The screenshots are what make it feel real.

**Length rule: if it's over two pages, you're writing a report instead of proof.**

---

## Rules

- **Give it away free with nothing owed.** No "book a call to get the full version."
  That gimmick is why most audit outreach fails.
- **Never say the word "audit" in the first email.** Say "a couple things I noticed."
  Audit sounds like a sales asset. It is, but it shouldn't sound like one.
- **Be right.** One wrong claim about their account destroys the whole thing. If you're
  unsure a finding is real, cut it.
- **Rank findings by dollars, not by effort.**

---

## Done when

- [ ] 3 completed audits exist as PDFs
- [ ] Each names at least one **specific, verifiable** problem — not generic advice
- [ ] Each is 2 pages or less
- [ ] Each has exactly one "do this week" action
- [ ] You called all 3 businesses and noted what happened

## Reusing audits as proof

Once a prospect acts on a free audit finding, you have a result to talk about — even
without a signed client. That line goes in touch 4 of the follow-up ladder:

> "The last HVAC company I sent one of these to turned off ads outside business hours
> and cut wasted spend by about a third in two weeks."

Only say it once it's true.
