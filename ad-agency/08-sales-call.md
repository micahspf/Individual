# Sales call script

30 minutes. Four parts. **Diagnose → Quantify → Prescribe → Close.**

Before the call: have their audit open, their live ads pulled up on your phone, and
Section 8 of the agreement ready to fill in.

---

## Part 1 — Diagnose (10 min)

**They talk. You take notes. Do not pitch.**

Open:
> "Before I show you anything — tell me how you're getting calls right now."

Then work through these. Shut up between each one.

1. "How do calls come in today — Google, referrals, Angi, trucks?"
2. "Have you run Google Ads before? What happened?"
3. "Who answers the phone, and what happens after 6pm?"
4. "How many trucks could you keep busy that you aren't right now?"
5. "What's the difference between a good month and a bad one for you?"

**Listen for the disqualifiers.** No capacity → they don't need leads. Phone rolls to
voicemail → fix that before selling ads. Burned by a previous agency → ask exactly what
happened, then explicitly contrast (you don't lock the account, they own everything).

## Part 2 — Quantify (5 min)

**Get two numbers. Do not proceed without them.**

> "Two quick numbers and I can tell you whether this makes sense.
> **One — what's your average job worth?** Repair and replacement separately if they're
> different.
> **Two — out of ten people who call you ready to book, how many actually book?**"

Then do the math out loud, on their numbers:

> "So a replacement is around $9,000 and you close about half the calls that come in
> for one. That means a booked replacement call is worth roughly $4,500 to you. If ads
> get you one for $250, that's the whole conversation."

**This is the pivot point of the call.** Once they've said their own numbers out loud
and heard the arithmetic, the retainer stops sounding like a cost and starts sounding
like a rounding error. Never say "ROI." Say their numbers.

Write the target cost per booked call into the agreement while they're on the phone.

## Part 3 — Prescribe (10 min)

Now show the audit. Three findings, ranked by money. Not five — three.

> "Here's what I'd do, in order.
> **First**, [biggest waste item]. That's money leaving today.
> **Second**, [landing/tracking fix].
> **Third**, [replacement keywords / schedule / whatever's next].
>
> Month one is build and tracking. We'll know something real by day 30. Not day 9 —
> day 30. I want to be straight with you about that up front."

Then say the ownership line before they ask:

> "One thing you should know: the account is yours. I build inside your Google Ads
> account, you're the owner, and if we stop working together you keep everything —
> campaigns, history, all of it. I've seen agencies hold accounts hostage. I don't
> do that."

## Part 4 — Close (5 min)

State the price. **Then stop talking.** The next person to speak should be them.

> "It's $1,000 a month for the first ninety days. I'm doing two of these at that rate
> in exchange for permission to use the results as a case study — after that it goes to
> $1,750. Ad spend is separate, budget's around $2,500 a month, and that goes straight
> to Google on your card. I never touch it.
>
> I'll send the agreement today. Once it's signed and the first month's in, I start
> the build. Sound good?"

If yes:
> "Great. Three things I need: agreement signed, first month paid, and admin access to
> your Google Ads account — or I'll create one if there isn't one. I'll send all three
> in one email in the next hour."

**Send it within the hour. Momentum dies overnight.**

---

## Objections — say it once, then move on

**"I already tried Google Ads and it didn't work."**
> "That's usually true and usually fixable. What was the monthly budget, and how long
> did you run it? … Yeah — at $600 a month for six weeks nobody could have made that
> work. There isn't enough data in that to optimize on. It's not that the channel
> failed, it's that it never got to the point where it could."

**"Send me a proposal."**
> "I can, but the audit I sent is basically it — the scope is on the last page. What's
> the piece you're unsure about? If it's the price I'd rather just talk about it now."

*A proposal request is almost always an unspoken objection. Find it on the call, because
you won't find it in email.*

**"Can you do our Facebook / website / SEO too?"**
> "No — I only do Google Ads for HVAC companies. That's on purpose. I'm better at this
> one thing than someone doing all five, and you'd rather I spend the month in your ad
> account than building you a website."

*Never say yes to this to save a deal. It is how the first client becomes unprofitable
by week three.*

**"That's more than I want to spend."**
> "Which part — the management or the ad budget? … If it's the budget, I'd rather wait
> until you can run $2,500 than take your money and produce a result neither of us can
> read. Can I check back in sixty days?"

*Walking away from an underfunded client is a profit decision, not a lost sale.*

**"Let me think about it."**
> "Of course. What's the piece you want to think about? — I ask because if it's
> something I can answer now, that saves you a week."

**"How do I know you can do this?"**
> "You don't yet. That's why the first two are at a discount and why the agreement is
> month-to-month after ninety days. Thirty days in you'll have real numbers, and if the
> cost per call isn't where we said, you'll know before you've spent much."

---

## Hard rules

- **No work before signature + payment.** Not the build, not the keyword research, not
  "just getting the account set up." Every unpaid head start ends the same way.
- Never negotiate the price down on the call. If they need a lower number, they get a
  smaller scope, not a discount.
- Never promise a lead volume or a cost per lead. You control the build; Google controls
  the auction.
- If they fail the ICP, end the call politely. A bad first client costs you the case
  study you're actually there to get.
