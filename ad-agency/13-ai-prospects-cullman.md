# Cullman prospect list — AI systems offer

The same 30-name method as [`02-prospecting.md`](02-prospecting.md), pointed at the AI
offer instead of ad management. Leave-behind:
`public/ai-systems-for-local-business.pdf`.

**Time: ~90 minutes to build the list. Then 10–20 touches a day.**

> Client cap is **4–6 at once**. Build the list to 30 anyway — most will not answer, and
> you want the pipeline full before you need it.

---

## Why Cullman is different from the ad-agency list

Over 80% of Alabama small businesses are non-employer firms, and Cullman's median
household income fell while poverty rose. Two consequences that change how you work
this list:

1. **Most of your prospects are one person.** They do not have a marketing budget line.
   They have a Thursday that disappears. Sell the Thursday back.
2. **You will meet them in person before you email them.** In a town of 19,000 the walk-in
   and the referral beat cold email, which is the opposite of the Huntsville ad play.

Also worth a pass: Cullman Chamber of Commerce member directory, and the vendor list
from Rock the South (the big local festival) — those businesses already spend on
marketing and are used to being approached.

## Step 2 — Score each one (the AI-offer ICP)

Different from the ad ICP. You are not looking for ad budget; you are looking for
**a repeated manual task plus enough volume to matter.**

| # | Test | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phone rings to a person during business hours — **call and check** | If nobody answers ever, they need Starter most and will feel it fastest |
| 2 | 2+ employees, or 1 person who is visibly slammed | Someone has to be losing time to a task |
| 3 | Real Google Business Profile with reviews | Proof they care about being found |
| 4 | Books appointments or quotes jobs | Booking and follow-up are the easiest wins to demonstrate |
| 5 | Been open 2+ years | Survived the risky part; has money |
| 6 | Owner is reachable and named | You are selling to a person, not a committee |

**Instant disqualifiers:** franchise with corporate systems · no phone listed · under
3.5 stars · closed more than open · already running a CRM they love (you would be
replacing, not adding).

## Step 3 — Write the observation (~20 min)

One sentence per row, specific enough to prove you looked. For this offer the strongest
observations are things **you personally experienced** as a would-be customer:

- "Called at 5:40 on a Tuesday, went to voicemail, no callback by the next morning."
- "Booking link on the Google profile 404s."
- "Their Google profile has 87 reviews and the last owner reply was 2023."
- "Contact form on the site has no confirmation — you cannot tell if it sent."
- "Two people told me they text the owner directly because the office line is hopeless."

**Call every single one before you approach them.** It takes 45 seconds, and the call
*is* the observation. That is the entire advantage of selling in a town this size.

---

## Step 4 — The approach, in order of what works here

### 1. Walk in (best)
Tuesday–Thursday, 9–11am or 2–4pm. Never Monday morning, never Friday afternoon, never
during a lunch rush.

> "Hey — I'm Micah, I run Individual over on [street]. I build small automation systems
> for businesses around here. I called y'all yesterday afternoon and it went to
> voicemail, which got me thinking. Can I leave this with you? No pitch — my number's on
> the bottom if it's ever useful."

Leave the one-pager. **Do not pitch.** Do not ask for a meeting. Leave and let the paper
work. Note the date in the tracker and follow up in a week.

### 2. Referral
You already run a local business. Ask the people you buy from:

> "Who do you know that's drowning in the office side of their business?"

### 3. Phone
Call the number you already tested. If a person answers, you have a live conversation
about the exact problem you are selling against.

### 4. Email — last resort here
Use the scripts in [`03-outreach.md`](03-outreach.md), swapping the ad observation for
an AI one. In Cullman this converts worse than walking in.

---

## Step 5 — What to sell first

**Always Starter or the $75 one-task.** Never lead with Full Custom.

The winning first sale in this market is almost always the same thing: *the call they
missed.* It is concrete, they already know they have the problem, and you can show it
working within a week.

> "What happens to a call at 7pm on a Saturday right now? … Right. That's the one I'd
> fix first. $300 a month, and you'd have it running inside a week."

If they flinch at $300, drop to the one-time build at $75–250 rather than discounting
the monthly. **Never discount the monthly rate** — it sets the number for every renewal
and every referral they send you.

## Daily minimums

| Metric | Floor |
|---|---|
| New businesses approached (walk-in, call, or email) | 5–10 |
| Follow-ups | 5 |
| Improvement to the offer or the one-pager | 1 |

Lower than the ad-agency floor of 10–20 because walk-ins take longer than emails and
the total pool is smaller. Quality of contact is higher too.

---

## Done when

- [ ] 30 rows pass the ICP
- [ ] All 30 called during business hours, result recorded
- [ ] All 30 have a specific observation from that call
- [ ] Sorted: voicemail/no-answer at the top — they need Starter most
- [ ] 10 one-pagers printed and in the truck

## Then

Work the top 10 by walking in. Log every visit. Ask for the sale on the second contact,
not the first — in a town this size the relationship closes the deal, and being pushy
on day one costs you the referral network that actually matters.
